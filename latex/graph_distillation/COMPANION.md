## 1. The paper in one paragraph (new thesis)

We build a distillation loss from attribution graphs: differentiable, neuron-level, with functional supernodes that align an 8B teacher and a 1B student. On two-digit addition it produces a striking held-out result: multiplication accuracy goes from 0.44 to 0.95 over three seeds, above the teacher's 0.89. We then use interpretability tools to find where that gain lives, and it is not where the method intended. Under the training protocol, the graph term is the only loss computed on inputs formatted as they are evaluated (with BOS). All of the multiplication gain is one decision at the answer position, whether to emit a number or a hedge (`?`). Seed-matched students are indistinguishable once evaluated in the format the KD term trained on. The decision is carried by layers 0–7 at the `=` position, and the student's routing is no closer to the teacher's than an SFT student's. A one-line loss that only rewards committing to a number reproduces the gain, and a scrambled graph target reverses it. The broader lesson is for distillation and fine-tuning research: an auxiliary objective's apparent generalisation can come from where it is computed, not from what it matches, and interpretability tools can tell the two apart cheaply.

**Title options** (the current one promises the transfer we no longer claim):
- *Where the Gain Lives: Auditing an Attribution-Graph Distillation Objective*
- *Graph Distillation, Audited: A Mechanistic Objective That Taught the Answer Format*
- *What Did the Circuit Loss Teach? Interpretability as a Check on Distillation Gains*

## 3. Abstract, introduction and the text that has to go

### 3.1 Abstract (≈ 200 words, fill in the voice)

1. Distillation objectives grade the student on outputs or hidden-state vectors, never on the causal structure of its computation. Attribution graphs make that structure explicit.
2. We introduce graph distillation:
   - differentiable attribution graphs over MLP neurons;
   - functional supernodes (ANOVA over a probe grid) that exist identically in two models of different size;
   - a JSD on supernode routing added to KD.
3. Distilling Llama-3-8B into Llama-3.2-1B on two-digit addition, it lifts a held-out multiplication family from 0.44 to 0.95 (three seeds), faster than every baseline, and keeps the gain.
4. **Turn:** we audit the gain with interpretability tools.
   - It is one answer-or-hedge decision at the answer position, in the input format only the graph term was computed in.
   - Seed-matched students are otherwise identical.
   - The decision lives in layers 0–7 at the final prompt token.
   - The student's routing is no closer to the teacher's than a label-trained student's.
   - A content-free loss that only rewards committing to a number reproduces the gain; a scrambled graph target reverses it.
5. Lessons: mechanistic training objectives should be checked with the same tools that motivate them; generative arithmetic evals should report commitment separately from correctness.

### 3.2 Introduction (five paragraphs)

**¶1 The gap.** KD matches outputs \citep{hinton2015distilling}. Its refinements match intermediate representations \citep{romero2015fitnets,sun2019patient,jiao2020tinybert}, or change which distribution and which samples are matched \citep{gu2024minillm,agarwal2024onpolicy}. None constrains *how* the student computes. Close the paragraph with the known puzzle that students can generalise well while matching the teacher poorly \citep{stanton2021does}, so what a student inherits is not what the objective names. That sets up the audit.

**¶2 The opportunity.** Attribution graphs describe computation concretely enough to put in a loss \citep{ameisen2025circuit,lindsey2025biology}. Two recent objectives already match mechanisms rather than outputs \citep{wu2022causal,wadhwa2025circuit}. Arithmetic is where LM mechanisms are best studied, including in our teacher's own family \citep{stolfo2023mechanistic,nikankin2025arithmetic,zhou2024pretrained}, and the published attribution-graph case study is the same "36+59" prompt we use \citep{lindsey2025biology}. So it is the natural first test.

**¶3 What we built and saw.** State the method in one sentence and the striking result: 21_mult 0.44 → 0.95, three seeds, peaks early, keeps the gain, and beats SFT, TinyBERT and CKA. Say plainly that this looks like transferred competence.

**¶4 The turn.** "We then asked where the gain lives." Summarise the five findings in the abstract's order, with the one number that carries each:
- KD's accuracy when it answers is 0.947 vs 0.950;
- no-BOS gap < 0.005;
- layers 0–7 give 0.945;
- patching at `=` gives 0.99;
- commit control 0.951 ± 0.005.

**¶5 Contributions.**
- (i) The method, including the cross-model functional supernodes, which stand on their own for reading supergraphs (Fig. 2).
- (ii) An audit protocol that transfers to any auxiliary training objective: split accuracy into commitment × correctness; evaluate in each input format the losses saw; transplant layers and patch positions between seed-matched students; run a content-free control.
- (iii) The finding itself, and its mechanism for the BOS interaction: the teacher's own hedge, copied by KD.

## 4. Related work (≈ 0.6 page, four paragraphs)

All keys are verified and defined in the merged `references.bib` (§9). Order matters: finish on the paragraph that sets up the audit.

**¶1 Distillation beyond outputs.**
- Soft labels \citep{hinton2015distilling}.
- Hints and intermediate representations \citep{romero2015fitnets,sanh2019distilbert,sun2019patient}.
- Attention maps and relations: attention transfer \citep{zagoruyko2017attention}; relational KD \citep{park2019relational}; MiniLM's self-attention distillation \citep{wang2020minilm}.
- TinyBERT \citep{jiao2020tinybert} and CKA \citep{kornblith2019similarity}, our baselines.
- Distribution and sample choices for LMs \citep{gu2024minillm,agarwal2024onpolicy}, and decoupled KD \citep{zhao2022decoupled}.

Point: all of these match the teacher's vectors or relations between them, never the causal edges between components.

**¶1b Distilling mechanisms (the closest prior work; must be discussed explicitly, not only cited).**
- **Causal distillation** \citep{wu2022causal} (DIITO) adds interchange-intervention training, so the student imitates the teacher's *causal dynamics* under aligned interventions. It is the earliest "match causal structure, not outputs" objective.
- **Circuit Distillation** \citep{wadhwa2025circuit} is the closest work to ours.
  - It maps teacher and student circuit components by how similarly ablating them affects the output.
  - It then aligns the representations of the mapped components.
  - It uses Llama-3 models on entity tracking and theory of mind, beats standard distillation, and updates only a targeted subset of parameters.
  - Same authors as `wadhwa2024mysteries`, and its PDF is already in the repo root (`2509.25002v1.pdf`).
- **Distilled Circuits** \citep{haskins2025distilled} is an analysis: GPT-2 → DistilGPT-2 students reorganise and drop teacher components while keeping behaviour.

**How we differ.**
- (a) What is matched: routing proportions in an attribution graph over functional supernodes, not component representations or intervention outcomes.
- (b) The alignment: automatic and functional (ANOVA), so it needs no ablation-based matching.
- (c) Most important for the new framing: we show that a mechanistic objective's held-out gain can come entirely from *where* it is computed. This is a check we suggest any mechanism-matching objective should run: evaluate in every input format the losses saw, and include a content-free control computed in the same place.

Phrase (c) as a methodological point, **not** as a claim that their gains are artifacts. We did not test their methods.

**¶2 What distillation actually transfers.**
- Students generalise without high fidelity to the teacher \citep{stanton2021does}.
- Studies of which knowledge properties do transfer \citep{ojha2023what}.
- CoT-augmented distillation works even when the rationale is placed after the label or is not coherent, so the supervised structure isn't what helps \citep{wadhwa2024mysteries}. This is the correct use of that citation, and it is directly analogous to our finding.
- Students inherit teacher biases and subgroup errors \citep{lukasik2022teachers}, and even traits hidden in the data when teacher and student share a base model \citep{cloud2026subliminal}. That is our case: Llama-3-8B and Llama-3.2-1B are closely related, and the student inherits the teacher's BOS-conditioned hedge under BOS-on KD.

**¶3 Circuits and attribution.**
- Causal mediation and patching \citep{vig2020investigating,meng2022locating,wang2023interpretability,heimersheim2024use,zhang2024towards}.
- Automated circuit discovery \citep{conmy2023towards}, attribution patching \citep{nanda2023attribution,syed2023attribution}.
- Dictionary features \citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,marks2024sparse}, transcoders \citep{dunefsky2024transcoders}.
- Attribution graphs \citep{ameisen2025circuit,lindsey2025biology}.
- Arithmetic specifically:
  - \citep{stolfo2023mechanistic}: attention moves operands to the last token, and MLPs write the result;
  - \citep{nikankin2025arithmetic}: a bag of heuristic neurons, **studied in Llama3-8B, our teacher**;
  - \citep{zhou2024pretrained}: Fourier features;
  - \citep{kantamneni2025trigonometry}: a helix/"clock" in Llama3.1-8B (a workshop paper; cite it as such);
  - \citep{feucht2026arithmetic}: base-10 addition neurons in Llama-3.1-8B, reused (preprint);
  - \citep{quirke2024understanding}: a toy transformer;
  - the Addition case study in \citep{lindsey2025biology}, on the same "36+59" prompt as our Figure 1.

  The Nikankin result is a good fit for our observation that the students' circuits never became teacher-like: the teacher's arithmetic is a distributed bag of heuristics, not a compact routing a JSD over six supernodes could transfer.

State our two departures: raw neurons instead of transcoder features, and automatic functional supernodes instead of hand-drawn ones.

**¶4 What training changes, and how evaluations mislead.** This paragraph sets up the audit.
- Fine-tuning often wraps or re-weights existing mechanisms rather than building new ones \citep{jain2024mechanistically,prakash2024finetuning}. Input-level shifts are best absorbed by early layers \citep{lee2023surgical}, which fits our layers-0–7 result.
- Model stitching and diffing compare networks by swapping parts or learning shared dictionaries \citep{lenc2015understanding,bansal2021revisiting,lindsey2024crosscoders,minder2025overcoming}. Minder et al. recover refusal-related directions in chat-tuning, the closest analogue to our hedge decision.
- The BOS token acts as an attention sink that models depend on \citep{xiao2024efficient,gu2025attention}.
- The BOS token acts as an attention sink that models depend on; removing BOS at inference collapses performance in \citep{barbero2025why}.
- LLM scores are sensitive to prompt format: up to 76 points \citep{sclar2024quantifying,mizrahi2024state}. First-token probabilities can disagree with generated answers \citep{wang2024my}.
- Accuracy-only benchmarks reward committing over abstaining \citep{kalai2025why}, and abstention is itself a learnable skill \citep{zhang2024rtuning}. Together these are the mechanism behind a "gain" that is only commitment.
- Shortcut learning \citep{geirhos2020shortcut,lapuschkin2019unmasking} is the general frame for our finding.

## 5. The new section: "Where the gain lives" (≈ 2.8 pages)

Open with one framing paragraph:
- the question is whether the student learned the teacher's computation or something else that raises exact-match accuracy;
- all analyses use the λ = 1 checkpoints at step 100, three seeds per arm, seed-matched (graph seed *s* and KD seed *s* share initialisation and data order);
- the code is in `src/experiments/{format_decomp,graph_delta,pos_patch,transfer_mechanism}.py`;
- the numbers differ slightly from Table 1, which uses λ = 3 at each seed's best in-distribution step. State this once: the audit uses λ = 1 at step 100 (graph 21_mult 0.949 ± 0.002 against Table 1's 0.951; KD 0.441 at step 100 against 0.512 at its best step). The draft's λ sweep (Table 4: λ = 1 gives 0.950, λ = 3 gives 0.951) makes the two directly comparable.

### 5.1 Commitment versus correctness

**Question.** Does the student compute the answer better, or just give one more often?

**Method.**
- Split greedy exact match into two parts:
  - **commit**: the continuation begins with an integer;
  - **accuracy-given-commit**: exact match among those.
- Add **forced accuracy**: the argmax over number tokens at the answer position equals the gold answer.
- Add **P(?)**: the next-token probability of tokens starting with `?`.
- The hedge is always "?\nTo find the answer…", the base model's instruct-style reply.

**Result: Table `audit_decomp` (21_mult; rows generated) and Figure `audit_decomp` (left panel).**

| 21_mult, BOS at eval | acc | commit | acc given commit | forced | P(?) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| teacher | 0.894 | 0.907 | 0.986 | 0.973 | 0.187 |
| base | 0.124 | 0.128 | 0.969 | 0.340 | 0.614 |
| KD (3 seeds) | 0.441 ± 0.051 | 0.466 ± 0.060 | 0.947 | 0.542 | 0.260 |
| SFT (3 seeds) | 0.715 ± 0.050 | 0.894 ± 0.050 | 0.800 | 0.514 | 0.079 |
| graph (3 seeds) | 0.949 | 0.999 | 0.950 | 0.859 | 0.062 |

**Claim sentence.** The entire KD-to-graph gap on 21_mult is commitment: when the KD student answers, it is as accurate as the graph student.

**Supporting detail (per-prompt, seed 1).**
- On the 579 prompts KD refuses, the graph student is right on 93.3% and the teacher on 89.5%, so the refused prompts are not intrinsically hard.
- KD's refusals rise with carries: its accuracy is 0.86 at 0 carries and 0.20 at 2+.
- Graph and teacher errors almost never coincide: 0 of 1 on 21_mult, 7 of 38 on 222_add, 1 of 16 on 33_add. There is no fingerprint of the teacher's computation.

**Caveat to state.** Forced accuracy differs (0.54 vs 0.86): in its hedging mode KD's number distribution is also worse. This is part of the same BOS-format effect (§5.2), not evidence of arithmetic learned in the other format.

**On addition** (appendix table, all families in `format_decomp/summary.json`):
- KD already commits on ≥ 98% of prompts, so the graph gain there is in correctness: 222_add 0.473 vs 0.438 given commit, and 33_add 0.656 vs 0.587.
- §5.4 shows a content-free control reproduces those too.

### 5.2 The same model outside the evaluation format

**Method.** Evaluate every checkpoint with and without BOS. Without BOS is exactly the format the KD and SFT terms trained on.

**Result: Figure `audit_decomp` (right panel), plus the seed-matched table.**

| seed | KD 21_mult | graph 21_mult | KD 222_add | graph 222_add | KD 22_add | graph 22_add |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.361 | 0.363 | 0.235 | 0.230 | 0.915 | 0.916 |
| 2 | 0.346 | 0.343 | 0.244 | 0.242 | 0.926 | 0.926 |
| 3 | 0.353 | 0.351 | 0.250 | 0.249 | 0.932 | 0.930 |

With BOS, 21_mult is 0.404 / 0.499 / 0.420 for KD against 0.949 / 0.948 / 0.951 for graph.

**Claim sentence.** The graph gradient changes the weights (§5.3) but not the function on the training format. Its whole behavioural effect is conditional on BOS, the only context it is computed in.

**Side facts worth a footnote.**
- Without BOS the base model is broken (22_add 0.013; it echoes the prompt), consistent with attention-sink dependence \citep{xiao2024efficient,gu2025attention}.
- The teacher without BOS is weaker (22_add 0.836, 21_mult 0.767), so BOS-free KD distils a degraded teacher.

### 5.3 Localising the change: layers, then position

**Layer transplant (Figure `audit_localise`, left).**
- **Method.** Copy the graph student's weights for layers 0..k−1 into its seed-matched KD student, then score 21_mult with BOS.
- **Results.**
  - Layers 0–7 reproduce the full effect: 0.945 / 0.945 / 0.948 against 0.949 / 0.948 / 0.951 for graph.
  - Layers 8–15 give 0.465 / 0.573 / 0.489 (KD alone: 0.404 / 0.499 / 0.421).
  - No single attention or MLP block is sufficient; the best is L0 MLP at +0.07.
  - Copying any single KD block into the graph student never removes the effect, so it is distributed and redundant within layers 0–7.
- **Weight difference.** ‖W_graph − W_KD‖ / ‖W_KD − W_base‖ is 0.24–0.37 in layers 0–2 and falls to 0.06 at layer 15.
- Cite the stitching methodology \citep{lenc2015understanding,bansal2021revisiting}.

**Residual patching (Figure `audit_localise`, right).**
- **Method.**
  - Run KD on 21_mult; every prompt tokenises to [BOS, a, *, b, =].
  - Overwrite the residual stream after layer L at one position with the graph student's.
  - Measure KD's commit rate.
- **Results.**
  - Patching at `=` after L7 gives 0.99 (seed 1; KD alone 0.42). Seed 2: 0.999 (KD 0.54).
  - BOS, a, * and b do nothing at any depth: all within 0.07 of KD.
- Cite \citep{meng2022locating,vig2020investigating,heimersheim2024use,zhang2024towards}.

**Claim sentence.** The graph term's supernodes span operand and sum neurons across positions, yet what it changed is a single decision computed at the final prompt token by the first half of the network. That is the signature of an output-format change, the "wrapper" that fine-tuning typically learns \citep{jain2024mechanistically}, not re-routed arithmetic.

### 5.4 Controls: what else produces the gain

**New arms,** all seed 1 unless noted and identical to KD otherwise:
1. **Scrambled graph target**, the draft's planned control: `--scramble-teacher-graph`, λ = 1.
2. **KD + commit loss**, −log Σ P(number tokens) at the answer position, computed on the batch *with BOS prepended*, weight μ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1}. It says nothing about which number.
3. **KD + gold CE** on the BOS-prefixed batch, μ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1}.

Flags: `standard_kd --bos-aux {commit,ce} --lambda-bos-aux μ`.

**Result: Figure `audit_controls` and Table `audit_controls` (rows generated). BOS at eval, step 100.**

| arm | 22_add | 222_add | 2222_add | 33_add | 21_mult |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KD (3) | 0.984 | 0.429 | 0.115 | 0.585 | 0.441 |
| graph (3) | 0.999 | 0.473 | 0.128 | 0.656 | 0.949 |
| scrambled graph | 0.158 | 0.000 | 0.001 | 0.033 | 0.018 |
| commit 0.01 (3 seeds) | 0.999 | 0.522 | 0.140 | 0.679 | **0.951 ± 0.005** |
| commit 0.1 | 1.000 | 0.557 | 0.151 | 0.706 | 0.966 |
| gold CE 0.1 | 1.000 | 0.579 | 0.166 | 0.756 | 0.971 |

- Every control is 0.36–0.37 on 21_mult without BOS, like KD.
- Seeds 2–3 of commit 0.01 were read from the training log: 21_mult 0.945 / 0.954, 222_add 0.518 / 0.527. The generated table has seed 1 only; add a "3 seeds" note or hand-enter the mean.
- **Scrambled run in detail:**
  - gradient share at step 1 / 50 / 100: 0.038 / 0.046 / 0.090, against a mean of 0.044 for the real target, so it is magnitude-matched;
  - its own graph loss stays at 0.36;
  - its loss against the real target rises from 0.058 to 0.091;

**Claim sentence.** A loss that carries no information about arithmetic, computed in the evaluation format, reproduces the graph term's gain on every family (three seeds) and exceeds it at higher weight. The graph target's content matters only in direction: the real target moves the answer-or-hedge switch toward answering, the scrambled one toward hedging. This fills the draft's "Is the content of the target what acts?" paragraph and Figure 4 slot.

### 5.5 Why the standard BOS convention removes the effect

**Numbers** (`format_decomp/summary.json`, teacher):

| Teacher P(?) at the answer position | 22_add | 222_add | 33_add | 21_mult |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| with BOS | 0.255 | 0.220 | 0.102 | 0.187 |
| without BOS | 0.004 | 0.002 | 0.001 | 0.001 |

**Mechanism.**
- Under `--bos-mode on`, KD trains the student to match that BOS-context distribution, including the hedge.
- On held-out families, where the student's number distribution is flatter, the hedge wins. That is why BOS-on KD scores 0.390 on 21_mult against 0.512 under BOS-free training.
- Under BOS-free training the KD term never sees the BOS format, so the graph term (or any eval-format loss) is unopposed there.
- The draft's own BOS-on table fits: graph at λ ∈ {1, 3, 10} gives 0.386 / 0.361 / 0.287. It does not beat a KD term that is actively training the hedge.

This replaces "we have no mechanism for it and offer none". The draft's "Hyperparameter" comment in `add_kd_args` (τ = 2 made the student emit `?` within five steps) is the same mechanism and worth a footnote.

### 5.6 Routing and circuit probes (half a paragraph in the main text, table in the appendix)

**Routing distance to the teacher** (mean JSD of routing profiles, held-out 22_add, ANOVA supernodes; lower is closer):

| student | JSD to teacher |
|---|---|
| base | 0.069 |
| KD | 0.050 |
| SFT | 0.046 |
| graph | 0.044–0.051 |

- Member-noise floor: 0.004–0.005.
- On the arg-token + DLA construction, which the student was not trained on, the graph student is no closer than any other (differences ≤ 0.003, noise ≈ 0.008).
- Same-prompt vs shuffled-prompt distances are close, so the target is nearly prompt-invariant. This matches the draft's appendix note.

**Circuit probes** (22_add, with BOS; `transfer_mechanism/summary.json`):

| probe | teacher | 1B students |
|---|---|---|
| operand-units carry patch | 0.48 | 0.23–0.28 (base 0.26) |
| sum carry patch | 0.79 | 0.90–0.92 |
| top-10 sum-range sharpness | 0.85 | 0.20–0.38 (graph 0.37 ≈ SFT 0.38) |

- Random-neuron controls stay ≤ 0.02.
- Zeroing the arg supernodes: teacher 0.98 → 0.59, graph 1.00 → 0.985.

**Claim sentence.** Any fine-tuning on 22_add moves the routing toward the teacher by the same amount, and no student acquires the teacher's localisation.

### 5.7 Discussion paragraph (end of §5, or its own short section)

1. **Why the method looked like it worked.**
   - The graph term was the only gradient in the evaluation format.
   - The effect peaked early (the untrained student's 22_add errors are 87% hedges; the graph student fixes them by step 10, inside warmup).
   - It beat representation baselines that could not act in that format.
   - Each is a reasonable-looking signal of real transfer.
2. **The general lesson for auxiliary objectives.** Check which input format each loss sees; split accuracy into commitment and correctness; evaluate in every format the losses saw; run a content-free control computed in the same place as the auxiliary loss.
3. **What a positive result would need.** A setting where the routing loss helps under one consistent format (BOS everywhere, answer-only KD, or a teacher without a hedge), and a second task.

## 6. Limitations and conclusion

**Limitations.**
- One task and one teacher–student pair.
- The audit uses λ = 1 checkpoints (the sweep suggests λ ≤ 6 behaves the same).
- The scrambled run and the μ > 0.01 controls are single seeds.
- The commit loss needs a task-specific notion of "number token", as the supernode categories need task-specific roles.
- The hand-written supernode categories still limit the method.
- Edge sign is discarded by the normalisation.

**Conclusion (four sentences).**
1. Attribution graphs can be turned into a differentiable, cross-model training signal.
2. On arithmetic, that signal produced a large, reproducible held-out gain.
3. Interpretability tools traced the gain to one answer-format decision in the evaluation context, which a content-free loss reproduces.
4. Mechanistic objectives deserve mechanistic audits, and the audit here costs minutes.

## 8. Anticipated reviewer questions

| Question | Answer and evidence |
|---|---|
| Isn't λ = 1 different from your main λ = 3? | The sweep shows at most 0.03 variation for λ between 1 and 6. The analysis's conclusions rest on seed-matched comparisons, which exist at λ = 1. |
| Maybe KD lacks competence on the prompts it refuses? | The graph student and the teacher are right on 93% / 90% of exactly those prompts (§5.1). Without BOS, KD and graph match within 0.005 (§5.2). |
| Is the scrambled collapse just a larger gradient? | No: the gradient share is 0.038–0.090 against 0.044 for the real target. |
| Why not just train with BOS? | We did (the draft's BOS-on table): the gain disappears, and §5.5 explains why. |
| Does your commit loss leak the answer? | No: it is summed over all number tokens. The gold-CE variant, which does, is reported separately and does only slightly better. |
| Is exact match with a 5-token budget fair? | Yes, and the split into commit and correctness makes the metric's behaviour explicit. |
| How is this different from Circuit Distillation \citep{wadhwa2025circuit} or causal distillation \citep{wu2022causal}? | Different matched object (attribution-graph routing over functional supernodes, not mapped-component representations or intervention outcomes). Our main contribution is the audit, which applies to any mechanism-matching objective: evaluate in every input format the losses saw, and run a content-free control in the same place. |
| If the gain is only answer format, why does it depend on the supernode size and on λ (Tables 4–5: 3 neurons per label gives 21_mult 0.46–0.74; λ = 0.3 gives 0.854)? | Because the graph term is a switch whose direction and strength depend on the target. The scrambled target pushes to hedging (0.018); a thin 3-neuron target pushes weakly or inconsistently; λ = 0.3 owns under 2% of the update and only partly flips it. The commit control shows how little push the switch needs when it points the right way (μ = 0.01). To make this airtight, decompose one n = 3 checkpoint's 21_mult errors into commit and correctness; the prediction is that its shortfall is hedges. |
| Did you fish for a mechanism? | The analyses were fixed before the controls ran, and the controls were predicted by §5.1–5.3 before they finished. |
